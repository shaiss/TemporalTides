# Writing Guidelines

**Part VI: Narrative Design**

---

## Tone and Voice

**Overall Tone:**
- Thoughtful but not heavy
- Wonder-filled
- Empathetic
- Empowering
- Honest (doesn't shy from difficulty; doesn't wallow)

**Genre Blending:**
- Science fiction (temporal mechanics)
- Superhero (abilities)
- Character drama (emotional depth)
- Philosophical fiction (reality, perception)
- Ensemble adventure (team)

**Balance:** Character focus never lost to spectacle.

---

## POV Rules

**Primary:** Third-person limited (one character's perspective per chapter/scene)
**Alternative:** First-person for intimate moments (optional)
**Head-hopping:** Avoid within scene; clear section breaks for POV shift

**POV by Character:**
- Each character has distinct internal voice (see Voice distinctions below)
- Perception shapes narration (Leo's tangential; Anne's anticipatory; etc.)
- Temporal experience reflected in prose pace

---

## Dialogue Style

**Principles:**
- Distinct voices (identifiable without tags)
- Show don't tell (personality through speech)
- Subtext (unsaid matters)
- Neurodivergent communication styles (authentic)

**By Character:** See [narrative-structure.mdc](../../.cursor/rules/narrative-structure.mdc) for dialogue techniques (Leo's literary refs; Anne's contingency planning; Maya's precision; Eli's rapid bursts; Dave's pauses; Iris's metaphor; Vibrion's electrical language)

---

## Prose Style

**Temporal Pacing:**
- Match prose pace to POV character's perception
- Leo: Varied; digressions; return to point
- Anne: Dense; anticipatory; multiple scenarios
- Eli: Short sentences; energy; present tense
- Dave: Heavier; pauses; musical rhythm

**Sensory Detail:**
- Ground in temporal zone (RFR affects sensation)
- Bioelectric when relevant (Dave, Vibrion)
- Avoid over-description; select telling details

---

## Restrictions (Never Do)

- Mental health for shock value
- Cure narratives
- Inspiration porn
- Savior complexes (neurotypical "fixing" neurodivergent)
- Violent mental health stereotypes
- Suffering Olympics
- Simplistic solutions
- Tokenization

---

## Should Do

1. ✅ **Show authentic experiences** - Based on real neurodivergent lives
2. ✅ **Celebrate differences** - Neurodiversity as genuine strength
3. ✅ **Normalize support** - Asking for and offering help is good
4. ✅ **Show complexity** - Both gifts and challenges of each condition
5. ✅ **Build community** - Neurodivergent people supporting each other
6. ✅ **Respect agency** - Characters make own choices about management
7. ✅ **Demonstrate growth** - Learning to thrive, not "overcome"
8. ✅ **Create empathy** - Help audiences understand different experiences

---

## Per-Condition: Show / Avoid

| Condition | Show | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Depression (Dave)** | The weight of time; difficulty seeing future joy; moments of connection breaking through | "Snapping out of it"; simple solutions; being "cured by friends" |
| **Anxiety (Anne)** | Racing thoughts; hypervigilance as strength and burden; managing not eliminating | "Just calm down" solutions; anxiety as purely negative |
| **ADHD (Eli)** | Hyperfocus as superpower; executive function challenges; energy management | "Just try harder" narrative; medication as plot device |
| **ADD (Leo)** | Deep dive thinking; difficulty with uninteresting tasks; rich inner world | "Lazy" characterization; focus as purely willpower |
| **Autism (Maya)** | Pattern recognition gift; sensory experiences; communication differences | Savant stereotypes; lack of emotion; "puzzle" metaphors |
| **Psychosis (Iris)** | Different relationship with reality; unique insights; managing symptoms | "Crazy" tropes; violence associations; losing all touch with reality |
